# DOPE Strategy Proposal Style

This file summarizes the reusable structure and style from the local gold-standard DOPE Strategy Proposal PDFs. Use it as a reference for future DOPE report jobs. Do not copy client-specific details from the examples unless that client is the current deal.

## Target Output

Prefer a concise, practical, sales-ready DOPE Strategy Proposal over a long AI research report unless the job contract explicitly asks for deeper research.

Default rhythm:

1. Cover page
2. Executive Summary / Objectives / Strategies & Scope
3. Campaign block continuation
4. Budget
5. Measurement & Reporting / Data & Setup / Optional Campaigns & Print / Next Steps

Target length: follow the active report contract first. In normal DOPE jobs, keep the proposal short enough for an account manager to review with a customer on a call: usually 3 to 5 skimmable sections/pages, not a long research report.

## Visual Rhythm

- Cover: full-page branded cover with the client logo/name large, monochrome industry/home/service image treatment, dark map/texture, DOPE logo near bottom, year at footer.
- Interior pages: repeat a red-tinted banner image across the top, then a large white content field.
- Typography: oversized bold condensed black headings; short heavy paragraphs; large bullets.
- Layout: plenty of white space, wide margins, oversized section titles, and gray campaign cards/tables.
- Color: primarily black, white, gray, and DOPE red. Avoid decorative gradients or generic AI visuals.
- Page rhythm: big heading, one concise explanation, then bullets or a gray boxed block. Keep pages scannable.

## Tone

- Direct, practical, and confident.
- Sales-ready, but not fluffy.
- Plain language a business owner understands.
- Write as DOPE Marketing making a clear plan, not as an analyst producing a generic market study.
- Favor specific operational language: audience, goal, trigger, timing, filters, cadence, tracking, budget, next step.
- Do not over-explain strategy theory.
- Do not invent client data. Use placeholders or call out needed setup when the deal payload lacks data.
- Use the DOPE /map data signals to make the report feel specific: industry, market, service area, status, website, logo/media, related deals, campaign preferences, and CRM/setup gaps.
- Make it personal and useful, not theatrical. Avoid strange metaphors, mascot voice, hype, or overly clever copy.

## Section Order

Use this order unless the job contract says otherwise:

1. Executive Summary
2. Objectives
3. Strategies & Scope
4. Budget
5. Measurement & Reporting
6. Data & Setup
7. Optional Campaigns & Print
8. Next Steps

## Executive Summary Pattern

Keep this short: 2 to 4 strong paragraphs or lines.

Pattern:

- "DOPE Marketing will help [Company] create a repeatable direct mail system that can be used to build consistency and measurable results."
- One sentence describing the campaign mix: automations, blitz campaigns, customer/reactivation campaigns, new homeowner/new mover campaigns, or seasonal campaigns.
- One sentence explaining that baseline data will be used to replicate successful strategies next quarter.
- CRM/source system line when known: ServiceTitan, Go HighLevel, HubSpot, CSV exports, or "CRM/source data to confirm."

## Objectives Pattern

Use 3 to 5 bullets.

Strong objective types:

- Build a baseline for measurable direct mail performance that can be repeated each quarter.
- Reactivate existing customers using CRM or customer list data.
- Target high-opportunity neighborhoods based on past job, home value, income, route, or service data.
- Automate mailers to neighbors of active job sites for ongoing visibility and local awareness.
- Use data and results to inform seasonal adjustments and the next campaign cycle.

## Strategy Block Pattern

Campaign blocks are the core reusable pattern. Use lettered headings:

- A) Brick by Brick - Automation
- B) Neighborhood Reactivation
- C) Affluent Homes / Premium Area Blitz
- D) Established Estates / Seasonal Blitz
- E) New Home Buyers / New Movers

Each block should include a one-sentence explanation, then a gray-box bullet list using this schema:

- Audience: who receives it
- Goal: what business result the campaign should drive
- Trigger: event or source condition when relevant
- Timing: launch window or start date
- Filters: geography, home value, income, customer segment, service type, route, or list rule
- Cadence: number of touches or mailings
- Tracking: matchbacks, QR, call tracking, CRM attribution, neighborhood-level reporting

Only include fields that make sense. If a field is unknown, write "to confirm" rather than inventing.

## Budget Style

Start with a simple cost explanation:

"The cost of a postcard includes design, print, postage and tracking."

Use simple, bold tables. Keep math easy to scan.

Useful budget blocks:

- Price Per Postcard
- Sample Costs
- Campaign row
- Homes
- Touches
- Total Cost
- Cost per home
- Total Cost (per job) for automation-style campaigns

Example table shape:

| Campaign | Homes | Touches | Total Cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Neighborhood Reactivation | 675 | 4 | $2,403 |
| Established Estates Blitz | 500 | 4 | $1,780 |
| Premium Area Blitz | 619 | 4 | $2,204 |
| Automation (Current Job Neighbors) | 25 | 4 | $89.00 per job |

Use real deal pricing when present. If pricing is missing, give a sample budget and label it clearly as sample or to confirm.

## Measurement & Reporting

Use bullets. Keep it operational:

- Main KPI: response rate by call, QR, form, or campaign touch.
- Reports: quarterly reporting with insights from CRM/source data and campaign matchbacks.
- Communication: monthly AM check-in and quarterly review to plan the next quarter.
- Attribution: matchbacks, neighborhood-level reporting, QR/payment/landing page activity, call tracking, and CRM status changes.

## Data & Setup

Use bullets that make required setup obvious:

- Preferred: connect CRM/source system with DOPE Marketing to enable automation.
- Customer Lists: request customer lists from the last 1-5 years.
- Option: provide CSV exports per location if full integration is not immediately available.
- Automation Setup: requires CRM/source integration for live job-triggered mailings.
- Confirm branding, service areas, locations, offers, approval contacts, and reporting cadence.

## Optional Campaigns & Print

Use this section to show DOPE can support more than direct mail without derailing the proposal:

- Print Items & Materials: door hangers, flyers, yard signs, leave-behinds, counter cards, seasonal pieces.
- DOPE Lead-Capture Pixel / Website to Postcard: identify visitors, match them to addresses, and send follow-up postcards when available and approved.
- New movers/new homeowner campaigns.
- Seasonal blitzes.
- EDDM or route saturation when targeting is less precise.

## Next Steps

Keep it decisive:

- Approve budgets.
- Confirm pilot markets.
- Confirm CRM/source-data path.
- Confirm branding and guidelines.
- DOPE prepares creative drafts, builds mailing lists, and activates campaigns.
- Set a meeting this week or next week to finalize details and start the quarterly strategy plan.

## Generation Rules

- Use the current deal/company data first. The examples teach format only.
- Do not copy client names, numbers, dates, geography, CRM, prices, or campaign specifics from the examples into unrelated reports.
- Prefer proposal pages and practical tables over long narrative sections.
- Keep recommendations tied to DOPE services: direct mail, postcards, print, EDM/email, landing pages, QR/payment links, CRM follow-up, and human approval.
- If the job contract explicitly requests a deeper market report, include the contract structure first and keep any deeper analysis concise. Do not append local legacy sections unless the contract names them.
